\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-background}

Graph neural networks have become a central modeling tool for collaborative filtering. Among them, LightGCN~\cite{he2020lightgcn} shows that a recommender can perform strongly with only normalized neighborhood propagation and layer-wise averaging, without nonlinear transformations or feature matrices. This simplicity makes LightGCN a strong collaborative baseline and a useful reference point for evaluating whether side information is truly beneficial.

Knowledge-graph-aware recommendation extends this setting by adding item-side semantics. Methods such as KGAT~\cite{wang2019kgat}, KGCL~\cite{yang2022kgcl}, MCCLK~\cite{zou2022mcclk}, and KGRec~\cite{yang2023kgrec} integrate KG structure through entity propagation, contrastive alignment, or rationale selection. These designs are effective when the KG is dense and reliable. However, real recommendation settings often rely on review-derived or domain-specific KGs whose coverage is sparse and uneven.

\section{Sparse-KG Observation}
\label{sec:intro-observation}

This work begins from a simple empirical observation. On the Amazon Books review-derived KG used in this study, each item has only $2.4$ aspect edges on average after filtering. Under the same split and evaluation protocol, all four KG-aware baselines fall below pure LightGCN on NDCG@20, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:intro-baseline}. The result does not imply that these baselines are weak in general; rather, it indicates that mandatory KG integration can become harmful when the KG is too sparse to provide stable semantic signal.

\section{Research Question and Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

The central question is how to use KG signal when it is helpful without letting KG noise contaminate the collaborative representation when it is not. RA-GARK addresses this by treating the KG as a gateable side channel rather than a mandatory component of the scoring path.

The contributions are threefold. First, we report a sparse-KG failure mode in which representative KG-aware methods underperform a no-KG LightGCN anchor. Second, we propose RA-GARK, a dual-view architecture with KG-SVD initialization, softmax rationale masking, and a local-biased fusion gate. Third, we show through main results, ablations, and a qualitative case study that KG semantics can improve ranking and reveal item characteristics when the architecture can regulate their influence.
